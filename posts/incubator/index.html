Incubator Presentation Summary⌗

Good morning, everyone.⌗

First and foremost, thank you for taking the time to join us.
I’m here to present a summary of our incubator and share what we’ve learned from the experience.

As part of our incubator, we worked on two simulations developed in Isaac Sim.

🌀 The First Project – Drift Simulation⌗

The objective of this project was to simulate realistic drifting behavior of a wheeled robot across different surface and steering conditions.

🧠 To explain this in simple terms:⌗

Imagine you went to Ladakh on a car, in that case scenario you might be driving on various surfaces like sand, ice, or concrete.
Each surface affects how the car turns or skids.
Similarly, our simulation replicates how a robot might behave under such varying conditions, helping us understand and train it better.

This simulation was built using the Wheeled Lab package available inside Isaac Sim.

Important Note:
The primary goal of the incubator was not 3D model development.
Instead, it was to generate synthetic data by training models to perform specific actions across different environments.

🔧 Technical Details:⌗

  • 3D models of the wheeled robots were imported in .urdf format.
  • The environment or scene was imported into the simulation using the .usd format.

🤖 The Second Project – SO-100 Arm: Pick Task⌗

The second project focused on simulating the SO-100 articulated robotic arm performing a basic pick task.

🎯 Objective:⌗

To have the robot identify and grasp a cube using articulation and grasping logic within a physics-enabled environment.

🔑 Key Features:⌗

  • Joint-based control for precise movement
  • Collision-aware contact sensors for accurate interaction
  • Grasping implemented using rigid body constraints

This setup lays a solid foundation for future exploration into learning-based approaches, such as reinforcement learning for autonomous manipulation tasks.
